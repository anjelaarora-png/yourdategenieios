## 2. The Service

### 2.1 Description

Your Date Genie is an AI-powered date-planning assistant. The Service helps you:

- Generate personalized date plan itineraries based on your preferences (location, vibe, food preferences, dietary restrictions, budget, special occasions, transportation, and dealbreakers).
- View 3 date plan options for each request, including venue recommendations, timeline, estimated duration, and budget.
- Create supplementary content: conversation starters, gift suggestions, and curated Spotify playlists.
- Save, organize, and archive date plans in a "Memory Gallery."
- Invite a partner to co-plan using the "Partner Planning" feature (see Section 5).

The Service is a *suggestion tool only*. We do not make bookings, reservations, or purchases on your behalf. You are responsible for verifying all information and making all reservations directly with venues or service providers.

3. **Partner Planning & Fraud Risk:**  
   Section 5 requires "informed consent" from the partner being invited to co-plan. However, if a user invites a partner without their knowledge and inputs fake preferences, is the consent language sufficient to shield us from fraud claims? Should we add a mechanism (e.g., email confirmation, or opt-in verification) to strengthen consent?

4. **Termination Without Cause — California Consumer Law Compliance:**  
   Section 11.2 allows us to terminate accounts "immediately and without notice" in broad circumstances. California consumer protection law (including CLRA and Unfair Competition Act) may restrict "arbitrary" termination of digital subscriptions. Should Section 11.2 require "reasonable cause" or "30 days' notice" for non-breach terminations?

5. **Data Retention & CCPA Compliance:**  
   Section 11.1 specifies 30-day deletion + 90-day backup retention. Does this align with CCPA deletion requirements (which require deletion of backups and recovering the data)? Should we clarify that "backups" are automated, encrypted, and not accessible to employees?

6. **Third-Party API Liability — Google Places:**  
   Section 2.2 and 8.2 disclaim liability for outdated or hallucinated venue info. If a user acts on a bogus Google Places address provided by our API and is harmed, are we liable to the user? Is the disclaimer sufficient? Should we indemnify Google Places or add a carve-out?

7. **OpenAI Sub-processor & AI Liability:**  
   Section 2.2 disclaims liability for AI outputs. However, if OpenAI's API generates discriminatory, biased, or defamatory content (e.g., a gift suggestion that is racially insensitive), are we fully protected by the disclaimer, or do we share liability with OpenAI? Recommend review of OpenAI's Terms of Service.

8. **EU/EEA Arbitration Carveout — Scope:**  
   Section 10.4 carves out arbitration for EU/EEA users but retains California governing law. Is this workable? Should we apply the law of the user's country of residence (GDPR principle) for EU/EEA users, or is California law acceptable as long as arbitration is waived?

9. **Competitive Misuse of Generated Plans (Section 4.6):**  
   We prohibit "reselling date plans" or "building competing databases" via scraping. However, if a user uses our plans for personal inspiration and references them in a blog, is that a violation? Should we narrow Section 4.6 to "commercial use at scale" rather than any secondary use?

10. **Age Verification & COPPA:**

Is a simple age attestation in signup sufficient, or should we implement a more robust age verification? (Note: COPPA applies to children under 13; we are targeting 17+, so COPPA may not apply, but state laws may.)
